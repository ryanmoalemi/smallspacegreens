Pick your seed.

Choose seed intended for edible microgreens, with the crop named and clear growing instructions. Radish suits a spicy topping; pea shoots have a milder, sweet-pea flavor.

The questions worth asking before you buy.

What will the next crop cost?

Check the replacement seed, mat, pod, or growing medium before choosing a kit. Count how many plantings the refill pack actually supplies. For loose seed, the seed calculator separates the seed you use from the whole bag you pay for. Add medium and other consumables separately.

Can I grow broccoli, radish, and sunflower with one setup?

Look for a method that supports the crops you want. A pre-seeded refill is tied to its supplied crop; a loose-seed setup offers more choice. Seed weights and preparation differ by crop, so use separate plantings and the crop-specific starting rates. A tray name alone does not tell you which seeds or medium are included.

Will one little kit give me enough for daily meals?

Do not assume it will. Harvest depends on crop, usable growing area, and conditions. Start with one tray, weigh the cut greens, and record how much you actually use. That gives you a better basis for planning repeat sowings than a photo of a full tray. We have not measured home-grown yields for individual kits.

How much work will I have to do?

Compare sowing, moisture checks, watering, harvest, and cleaning between crops. Pre-measured seed can remove a task, but read the full care instructions before choosing. Our daily check turns the growing process into a short routine without assuming every tray needs the same watering schedule.

Do I want microgreens or jar sprouts?

They use different methods. Microgreens are cut above their growing medium; jar sprouts are generally eaten with the seed and root. Check which result you want before buying a jar, tray, or kit. The first-tray guide explains the distinction and relevant food-safety guidance.

Which seeds should I buy?

Choose seeds intended for edible microgreens or sprouting, with clear crop and growing instructions. Penn State recommends high-quality seed that has not been chemically treated or coated. A low price or a generic bag of bird feed does not establish suitability for growing food.

What is a microgreens growing kit?

A kit is one purchase that includes a growing container plus seed and a growing medium, often with instructions. Read what the next planting needs. A kit and a bag of loose seed are different buys.

Does a microgreens growing kit include a lamp?

Do not assume it does. Some countertop gardens include a light in the unit. A tray kit may not. Compare the listing with the light you already have, and with the space above the tray.

How is a kit different from trays I reuse?

A kit decides more of the parts for you. Reusable trays leave the seed and the mix as separate choices. The supply list above follows the route you pick. This site has not grown the products in the cards.
