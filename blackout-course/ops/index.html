FOUR BUYER PERSONAS TO TEST

1. The practical homeowner

55–72 · Core test

Motivation: get the house in order without becoming a hobbyist prepper.

Objection: “I have supplies somewhere already.”

Creative: ordinary night-time power loss; 15-minute hands-on fix with a real home.

2. The responsible parent

40–60 · Family

Motivation: protect everyday routines when lights, water or stores fail.

Objection: “This is another course I won't finish.”

Creative: 30 small physical outcomes, not a giant shopping haul.

3. The adult-child gift buyer

35–55 · Gift

Motivation: help parents build easy-to-use home readiness.

Objection: “Will they be able to navigate the website?”

Creative: show big Play buttons, printed guide, simple daily steps.

4. The DIY self-reliance buyer

45–70 · Practical

Motivation: concrete useful improvements and gear checkups.

Objection: “Why buy what I can learn free?”

Creative: completion system, source-checked guides, no nonsense.

Segment exclusions to test

Renters, people with mobility constraints, pet-free or car-free households require substitute projects. Avoid implying one curriculum fits everyone.

Creative guardrails

Do not infer vulnerability from age, imply an imminent disaster, or use AI actors claiming to be real students. Focus on everyday competence and credible hands-on results.

Acquisition hypotheses

AD CHANNELS AND TEST ANGLES

Paid channel sequence

  1. Meta Facebook Feed: initial 45+ homeowner creative testing, mobile-friendly 4:5 and 9:16 assets. Use broad targeting and creative signals.
  2. Meta Reels / Instagram: reuse winning concepts with creator-style edits, not overproduced avatars.
  3. Google Search: test high-intent emergency preparedness course / printable kit queries once landing page converts.
  4. YouTube: later explainer and remarketing assets from actual demonstration footage.
  5. Creator partnerships / email: real homeowners, educators and household-organization creators. Verify rights, claims and affiliate disclosures.

Controlled first test

Budget hypothesis: $2,000 over about two weeks, subject to approval. Start with three angles × two real variants each, rather than 30 weak AI posts.

Success metrics: purchase CAC, activation, refund-adjusted contribution, Day 1 and Day 7 completion, and support tickets.

First funnel: ad → clear sample mission → honest material-cost disclosure → one-time checkout → Day 1.

An illustrative $1.25 link CPC and 2.5% purchase rate imply $50 CAC. Neither is a forecast. Avoid using generic traffic-CPC medians as purchase-CAC predictions.

EMAIL SEQUENCES AND AUTOMATION

Click a message to preview the copy, trigger and suppression rules. Use recipient time zone and selected cadence, not an assumed local clock. Marketing SMS requires separate opt-in.

Suggested providers: Resend or Postmark for transactional email; Customer.io, Loops or a custom queue for lifecycle events; Twilio for explicitly opted-in SMS. Do not send multiple daily nudges or sell contact information. Event-driven cancellation prevents emails after completion or opt-out.

Optional offers, cost verification required

UPSELLS THAT ADD ACTUAL VALUE

Printed spiral field guide

Illustrative $29–$39

One easy-to-read printed copy of the 30 photo-guides. No printer required. Test as a post-checkout offer only after quotes for printing, shipping, packaging and returns.

Gift another household

Illustrative $59–$89

A separate 30-day course account for another address. Strong fit for an adult buying for family. Real entitlement, redemption and refund logic needed.

Preparedness starter supplies

Not priced

Potential optional labeled supplies or common consumables bundle. Do not promise comprehensive safety equipment before supplier quotes and product-liability review.

Advanced course waitlist

No pre-sale

Level 2 projects can be a future offering, but should not rescue the economics of Level 1. Only open interest collection with consent.

Upsell restraint

Do not put the same basic PDFs, necessary video access, safety notes or earned recognition behind a second payment. Those are core promised benefits.

Economics logic

Incremental order value is not incremental profit. Measure attach rate, additional refund risk, COGS, picking, packaging, shipping, support and creator fees.

LAUNCH GATES & NEXT ACTIONS

Five workdays

Day 1: lock the final 30 distinct physical outcomes and equipment/materials matrix.

Day 2: produce drafts of missions 7–14, photos and safety check notes.

Day 3: missions 15–22, finish video script convention and captions.

Day 4: missions 23–30, consolidate dependencies and special household branches.

Day 5: editorial audit, photo/gear consistency, demo user test, pricing shortlist.

This sprint targets content drafts, not unverified claims that all 30 videos or field tests are finished.

Do not open sales until

  1. Course safety steps, photos and videos are checked against real equipment.
  2. Materials list and material-cost expectations are fully disclosed.
  3. Stripe purchase and secure membership access are tested.
  4. Physical pin, card, personalization and shipping are quoted.
  5. Refunds, support, content corrections and customer contact preferences work.
  6. Claims, testimonials and ad policy review are complete.
