Use the calculator first. Radish, pea, sunflower, broccoli, kale, arugula, cilantro, basil, and rutabaga use Utah State's grams per 10 by 20 inch tray. Mustard, beet, and amaranth do not. For those three, type a rate or explicitly choose Maryland's general 10 to 15 gram example. That example is not a crop trial.

Calculate seed for your tray, then check seed suitability and packet size.

A 10 by 20 inch tray is 200 square inches, the reference for the rates below. A 10 by 10 inch tray is half that area, so it gets half the seed. A 5 by 5 inch tray is one eighth. These are nameplate sizes. Measure the inside floor for the area that holds mix, and use custom size in the calculator.

Scale by area

Divide your tray's inside area by 200, then multiply the 10 by 20 inch rate. A 10 by 10 inch tray is half. A 5 by 5 is one eighth. Measure the inside floor, not the outer rim. Nominal size is not the growing floor.

Penn State's class used about 10 to 15 grams on a 12 by 20 inch 10-row tray, which is 240 square inches, and a count of about 6 to 8 large seeds or 10 to 12 small seeds per square inch. That lighter band is a different method. The calculator does not apply it. If a stand is thin or matted, change the next tray.

Weigh pea and sunflower dry, then soak. Utah State lists about 6 hours for pea and 12 for sunflower. Wet seed is not a reason to sow extra.

Evidence notes

Maryland's tablespoon, about 10 to 15 grams, is a sample broadcast for a 10 by 20 inch flat, plus a note that the flat holds about 1,500 to 4,500 seeds depending on size. Seed counts on that page include about 53 beet seeds per gram, about 281 broccoli seeds per gram, and about 530 mustard seeds per gram. A spoon changes weight when the seed changes. Illinois prints spoon amounts for a few small containers. They will not match the area scale seed for seed. Mississippi State gives about 2 to 3 tablespoons of radish per 10 by 20 inch tray, which is volume, not the Utah State gram rate. Penn State's density calculator can include germination. This site's tool does not.

FAQ

Why is pea so many more grams than arugula?

The seeds are heavier. Utah State lists 150 grams of pea and 10 grams of arugula for the same 1020 tray.

Can I use 10 to 15 grams for every crop?

That is Maryland's sample and, on a larger tray, Penn State's class rate. It is lighter than Utah State's rates for radish, pea, and sunflower. Know which one you are following.

What if germination is poor?

This calculator does not add a germination factor. Sow the published rate, look at the stand, and change the next tray. Penn State's tool can take germinability.

Do I soak radish?

Not in the Utah State table. Pea in that table is a soak. Weigh pea before it goes in the water.
