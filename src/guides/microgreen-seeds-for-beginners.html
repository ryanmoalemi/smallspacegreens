Buy one crop first. Microgreen seeds for a beginner are easier as a single named crop than as a mix you cannot separate. Radish is the lighter first tray in the Utah State table. Pea is milder and much heavier.

See which crop to buy first, or compare seed, a kit, and a jar.

The phrase microgreen seeds covers a lot of packets. Some are sold for the greens you cut above the mix. Some are sold for jar sprouts, which you eat whole, root and seed included. Those are different foods. Penn State recommends high-quality seed that has not been chemically treated or coated. A low price does not tell you the seed is meant for eating as a microgreen.

Which crop to buy first

Utah State prints a gram amount for a 10 by 20 inch tray. Radish is 42 grams, with no presoak in that table, and a harvest window of about 8 to 12 days. Broccoli in that table is 28 grams, also without a presoak. Pea is 150 grams, weighed dry, then soaked about 6 hours. Sunflower in that table is 48 grams, with a longer soak. Those numbers are a planning start. They are not a promise that a bag from any seller will finish on that day.

A first tray does not need the full 10 by 20 inch flat. A 10 by 10 inch tray is about half the area, so it needs about half the seed. Use the seed calculator for your tray, then check that the bag covers that dry weight. A 4 gram packet of radish will not sow a full 10 by 20 inch tray at 42 grams. Buy the size that matches the tray you will actually use.

Do not mix the crops in one tray. Illinois Extension splits faster crops from slower ones, and pea needs a soak that radish does not. One crop, one tray, one note of what you cut. The comparison of the two most common first crops is pea shoots vs radish.

Seed, a kit, or a jar

Loose seed is the right buy when you already have a tray and mix, or you are willing to choose them. A kit puts the container, the seed, and the medium in one purchase. The kit card points at True Leaf Market's kits collection.

Skip sprouting seed if you want microgreens. The FDA treats raw sprouts as a higher-risk food, including sprouts grown at home. Microgreens are still raw produce: wash your hands, the tool, and the cut greens. Washing reduces bacteria and does not eliminate them. If a tray molds, discard it. Do not try to dry it out and eat it.

Tomato, pepper, and eggplant seed are not microgreen seeds. Illinois Extension says not to eat those seedlings because of toxins in the stems and foliage. Keep them for the transplant guide if you are starting plants for a balcony.

FAQ

Which microgreen seed should a beginner buy first?

One named crop. Radish is the lighter sowing in the Utah State table and it has no presoak there. Buy a bag that covers the dry grams for your tray. A mix can wait until you know what one crop looks like.

How much seed is a first tray?

Use the crop's published rate and the inside area of your tray. Utah State's 10 by 20 inch radish rate is 42 grams. A half-size tray is about half of that. Weigh the seed dry before any soak.

Are sprouting seeds the same thing?

No. Sprouts are eaten whole after a warm wet germination. Microgreens are cut above the growing mix.

Does a small packet cover a full tray?

Often no. Read the gram weight on the bag and compare it with the calculator result. A packet photo is not a tray's worth of seed.
