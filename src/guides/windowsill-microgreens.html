Try one small tray on the brightest sill. If stems stay upright and colored, keep the window. If they lean hard or stay pale, add a lamp. Hours of sun are not the same thing as light intensity. A north window in winter is the weakest default.

Use the first-tray steps once the sill can hold the container.

A one-tray trial

  1. Measure the sill. A 5 by 5 or 10 by 10 inch tray often fits when a 10 by 20 inch flat does not. Scale the seed to that size.
  2. Sow one short edible crop, such as radish or arugula. Maryland says lettuce, arugula, mustards, and Asian greens tolerate weaker light better than many other crops.
  3. Germinate covered, off an icy pane. Uncover when the seed is up and put the tray in the brightest glass you have. Rotate it daily.
  4. Judge the plants, not the clock. Upright, colored stems can stay. Hard lean or pale color means add light. Di Gioia notes that a south window usually gets more sun than a north window.

When a lamp is the next step

Maryland suggests about 16 hours of light, with lamps typically 6 to 12 inches above the leaves. Utah State's guideline is 18 hours on and 6 off. Those are published targets for light in general, not a promise that your window matches them. Utah notes a winter day there can be as short as about 8 hours of sun.

Before buying, measure the tray and the space above it. Check the fixture's listed coverage at its recommended growing distance, allow room to adjust its height, and check whether it works with a timer. Maryland recommends an outlet timer for daily on and off times. Use the lighting supply checklist to check what you need.

Do not confuse this with transplants. Steve Reiners at Cornell says a south window is not enough for sturdy vegetable transplants kept for weeks. That warning is for the seed-starting guide. A microgreen is cut small, so a window is a fair first test.

Keep the reservoir tray on the sill so water does not run onto the wood. Empty it in the sink. Cold glass can stall germination. Move a covered tray back into the room if the pane feels icy, then return it to the light. Do not park a wet covered tray on a radiator.

FAQ

Is a north-facing window enough?

It is the weakest place to start. You can germinate there, then add a lamp. Do not expect the same color as a south sill in winter.

Which crops should I try first?

Radish or arugula. Long covers such as cilantro in the Utah State table are a weaker match for a dark winter sill.

Does a desk lamp count?

It depends on the bulb, distance, and coverage. Check those details rather than assuming every desk lamp can light a tray, and watch for heat on the leaves.

Why do transplant guides reject a window?

Those plants stay in pots for weeks and need stocky growth. Microgreens are harvested much sooner.

Evidence notes

Iowa State says microgreens are feasible indoors in winter with a sunny window. Di Gioia writes that light behind a window is generally enough, and that shoots lean when light is limited. Penn State calls microgreens a low-light crop that can finish under imperfect light because the cycle is short. Maryland is more cautious: a sunny window may suffice, and the crop generally grows better under lights. Use the lean of the stems as the decision, not a single sentence from one page.
