Two layouts

Layout A, about 3 hours of sun. Two 8 inch pots with one lettuce each, plus a small herb pot. Do not add radishes on that sun budget.

Layout B, about 4 hours or more. Layout A plus one 10 inch pot of radishes or scallions. Fruiting crops stay off both sketches unless you have about 6 to 10 hours.

UC Marin wants at least about 6 inches of depth. Every pot needs a hole. Empty the saucer. Do not put rocks in the bottom. Use container mix, not ground soil. The handout says if any topsoil is added, keep it to about 10 to 15 percent.

Weight, wind, and what this page does not claim

Check your building's rules and allowable balcony load before adding substantial planter weight. Include wet growing medium and stored water. Smaller pots are easier to move, but that does not establish how much weight the balcony can support. Secure pots so a gust cannot send them off a railing. These extension pages do not give a wind-speed rating or a pounds-per-square-foot limit, so this guide will not invent one.

Self-watering containers are a different tool. This page does not compare one, because no model has been checked here for reservoir size or refilling.

If the balcony fails the sun test, do not force a tomato. Grow leaves indoors as microgreens. Wash what you cut. The FDA says to rinse produce under running water, including food you grew, and not to use soap.

FAQ

How much sun does a salad need?

The UC ANR handout says leafy crops can do well with as little as about 3 hours, roots about 4 hours, and fruiting crops about 6 to 10 hours. Measure your balcony.

What pot should I start with?

An 8 inch pot for one lettuce, or a 10 inch pot for a small patch of radishes if you have the sun. All of them need a drainage hole.

Can I use soil from the ground?

No. The handout says ground soil is too heavy. Use a container mix.

What if the balcony is too shady?

Grow the leaves indoors. Do not add radishes to a 3 hour sill and call it the same plan.
