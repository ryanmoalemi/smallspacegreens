Before you buy a pot

Write down the space available for the whole container, including its saucer, and where you will stand to water it. Check how the door opens and where drained water will go. Record the hours of direct sun in the place the pot will actually sit. Start with one container you can manage, then use what you learn about watering, light, and harvest to plan the next one.

The 3-hour layout

Start with Layout A if you measured about 3 hours of direct sun. Two 8 inch pots hold one lettuce each, and a small herb pot sits beside them. Do not add radishes on that sun budget.

The 4-hour upgrade

If you later measure about 4 hours or more, add one 10 inch pot of radishes or scallions to Layout A. Fruiting crops still need about 6 to 10 hours of sun.

Pot and soil rules

UC Marin wants at least about 6 inches of depth. Every pot needs a hole. Empty the saucer. Do not put rocks in the bottom. Use container mix, not ground soil. The handout says if any topsoil is added, keep it to about 10 to 15 percent.

Make a supply list for your chosen layout: the selected crops, pots with drainage holes and saucers, and container mix. Check pot diameter and depth before ordering. If you are raising transplants indoors, plan when to sow them.

Balcony weight safety

Check your building's rules and the allowable balcony load before you add planters. Count wet growing medium and stored water. Smaller pots are easier to move. Secure pots so a gust cannot send them off a railing. The cited extension pages do not list a wind-speed rating or a pounds-per-square-foot limit.

If you have under 3 hours of sun

If the balcony gets less than about 3 hours of direct sun, do not force a tomato. Grow leaves indoors as microgreens. The seed density calculator helps you size a tray.

Wash what you cut. The FDA says to rinse produce under running water, including food you grew, and not to use soap.

FAQ

How much sun does a salad need?

The UC ANR handout says leafy crops can do well with as little as about 3 hours, roots about 4 hours, and fruiting crops about 6 to 10 hours. Measure your balcony.

What pot should I start with?

An 8 inch pot for one lettuce, or a 10 inch pot for a small patch of radishes if you have the sun. All of them need a drainage hole.

Can I use soil from the ground?

No. The handout says ground soil is too heavy. Use a container mix.

What if the balcony is too shady?

Grow the leaves indoors. Do not add radishes to a 3 hour sill and call it the same plan.
