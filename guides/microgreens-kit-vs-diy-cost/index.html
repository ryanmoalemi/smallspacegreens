Microgreens kit vs DIY: compare setup and repeat costs

Compare the complete setup, refill costs, and daily work. Enter your prices to compare what you pay today and what the next planting needs.

By Ryan Moalemi. Updated November 10, 2026.

Start with the whole cost. A kit may save decisions; reusable trays let you choose your seed and medium. Compare what you pay today, what each later planting needs, and the work you want to do. Neither route is automatically cheaper.

If you are trying one tray, a suitable clean food container may be enough. You can decide whether to buy a larger setup after your first harvest.

Checkout spend versus seed you actually sow

Count three different numbers. Checkout spend is what you pay now for whole packs. Consumed cost is the slice of seed one sowing uses. Equipment spread over many trays only helps if you say how many times you will reuse the tray.

University of Maryland Extension says seed in a 1/32 ounce packet can cost about 30 times more per seed than a quarter-pound bag. Compare the actual kit contents and refill prices before choosing: that packet example does not establish the cost of every kit. The calculator turns your own price per pound, or a package price plus package weight, into those two seed costs.

Will the harvest match what you eat?

A small starter kit may be a useful experiment without replacing your regular grocery purchases. Before scaling up, record your crop, growing area, harvest weight, and how much you used. Include failed or wasted plantings when you judge value. We do not have measured apartment yields for specific kits, so this guide does not promise a serving count or a payback date.

Convenience matters too. Compare preparing seed, sowing, checking moisture, harvesting, and cleaning between batches. Choose the routine you can keep up, not just the lowest advertised tray price.

The arithmetic is grams times the printed price per pound, divided by 453.59237. Pea is 150 times 3.78 divided by 453.59237, about $1.25. The $10.19 radish line is 42 times 10.19 divided by 453.59237, about $0.94. Utah State printed $1.16 and $0.88. This site does not change those printed figures. The gap is in the source table, not in the calculator. Pea does not automatically cost more per tray than every radish once you use your own price.

Evidence notes

Penn State's course sketch, which it says to adjust, put trays, peat-based mix, and fertilizer at about $0.67 per tray, and seed at a 10 gram sowing from about $0.47 to more than $2.21. Utah State's radish and pea rates are heavier than 10 grams, so that 10 gram line understates those crops. In Penn State's 2016 to 2017 greenhouse record, at a 10 to 15 gram sowing, arugula averaged about 148 grams per tray and cilantro about 47 grams. Those were greenhouse trays, not an apartment sill. Do not divide a kit price by those grams.

Round planning bands such as a kit at $15 to $40 are not on this page as a shopping list. They were placeholders, not verified baskets.

What to skip

Choose suitable, untreated seed intended for edible microgreens or sprouting. Do not treat cheap bird feed as an equivalent without verified suitability. Penn State recommends high-quality seed that has not been chemically treated or coated. Skip garden soil; Maryland recommends a soilless mix.

FAQ

Is a kit always more expensive?

No. Compare what the kit includes, what you already own, and the price and quantity of replacement supplies. Maryland's packet-versus-bulk example explains one possible seed-cost difference; it does not price a particular kit.

Why do the two columns in the dated example disagree?

One column is what Utah State printed on January 8, 2026. The other is this site's division using that page's grams and price per pound. Neither column is today's shop price.

Should the first budget include a lamp?

Only if the window is weak. A lamp is a one-time tool, not a per-tray seed cost. See the windowsill guide.

Where do I put my own price?

Enter kit and DIY prices in the setup comparison above. For seed quantities, use the seed calculator: price per pound estimates seed consumed, while package price plus grams estimates bags at checkout.
