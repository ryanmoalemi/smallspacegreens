How to bottom water microgreens and seed trays

Give roots water from below, let the mix absorb it, then pour out the extra.

By Ryan Moalemi. Updated November 10, 2026.

Do this. Nest a tray with holes in a tray without holes. Add a shallow layer of water to the solid tray. Remove the growing tray and pour out the extra once the mix is moist.

Steps

  1. 1
    Fill the drained tray with moist mix about 1 to 1.5 inches deep, or with coir. Level it.
  2. 2
    Set it in the solid tray. Add a shallow layer of water. Utah State's coir example uses about 2 cups in a 1020, then pours off the leftover after about 30 minutes.
  3. 3
    A Cornell seed-starting handout says no more than about an hour in roughly an inch of water. Those times are examples, not one timer for every mix.
  4. 4
    Lift the growing tray out and empty the solid tray. Do not leave roots sitting in a puddle.
  5. 5
    Mist only while new seed still needs a wet surface. After roots hold the seed, return to bottom watering when the tray feels light.

If the crop molds

If the crop has mold, discard it and clean and sanitize reusable trays before starting again. Changing the watering or airflow does not make a moldy crop suitable to eat. LSU AgCenter says any crop that grows mold should be discarded and the trays sanitized. Root hairs are commonly mistaken for mold. Unusual growth that migrates up the stems is mold. If you are unsure, discard the crop.

For the next sowing, empty the reservoir sooner and use fresh mix if the last tray rotted. Maryland allows a half-and-half refresh only after old roots have dried. Iowa State ties root rots and damping-off to overwatering.

Rocks in the bottom of a pot are not drainage. UC sources say a hole is what lets water leave. On a windowsill, empty the solid tray in the sink.

FAQ

How long should a tray sit in water?

Until the mix is moist, then out. Utah State's coir example is about 30 minutes. A Cornell handout says no more than about an hour. Pick the short end if the mix is already dark.

Can I bottom water a tray with no holes?

No. Water has to enter through holes, and extra water has to leave. The solid tray is only the reservoir.

Should I add gravel?

No. The UC container handout says rocks do not replace a drainage hole.

Is misting the same thing?

No. Mist the surface for the first days. Bottom water after the mix can wick, so the leaves stay drier.

Evidence notes

Penn State says sub-irrigation keeps leaves drier, which matters for disease, and tells home growers to remove the tray once the mix is wet. Di Gioia says to wet mix from below before sowing and to avoid extra moisture that leads to mold. Maryland says mist first, then water from the bottom or the top, moist but not saturated. Illinois says water lightly from the bottom after germination. Mississippi State points to bottom watering on a kitchen sill and warns that a hard flood can wash seed off a mat.
